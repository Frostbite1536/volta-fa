## 1. Small-message all-reduce over PCIe P2P (Phase 3)

### Mechanism

The mechanism is a one-shot push collective (`custom_all_reduce.cuh`), adapted, by its own comment, from SGLang-V100
(`haohervchb/sglang-V100@845b9fdf7a7e`).
- **Push:** each GPU writes its partial sums directly into every peer's receive slot through P2P pointers.
- **The data is the signal:** slots are pre-filled with a reserved FP16 NaN pattern (`0x7f7f`, set by
  `cudaMemset` with `0x7f`), and receivers poll the payload itself until it is no longer the sentinel. There is no
  separate flag write and no extra round trip.
- **Collisions:** a payload value equal to `0x7f7f` is first rewritten to the canonical NaN `0x7e00`, so every
  finite value, signed zero included, passes through unchanged.
- **Two epochs:** two slot sets alternate, so re-arming one overlaps with using the other.
- **Size gate:** at most 80 KiB (`VLLM_SM70_PCIE_SWITCH_CAR_MAX_BYTES=81920`), and only inside CUDA graph
  capture (`VLLM_SM70_PCIE_SWITCH_CAR_GRAPH_ONLY=1`). Larger payloads and eager calls use NCCL.
- **Topology gate:** the mode turns on only for 4 sm_70 GPUs that are pairwise PIX/PXB (one PCIe switch) with
  working P2P (`_sm70_pcie_switch_tp4_mode`). With 2 GPUs, ReVolta uses vLLM's upstream custom all-reduce.
- **Fusion prototype:** one kernel does the peer reduction, residual add and RMSNorm
  (`sm70_peer_reduce_gemma_rms_norm`), keeping the normal peer summation order.

### Why it matters, per ReVolta's own figures

- The maintainer's estimate (r/v100 thread, not a measurement) is ~100 all-reduces per decode step at TP4: about
  25 KB each at 1 request and 200 KB at 8. At these sizes latency matters more than bandwidth.
- Their PCIe-switch box reaches 201 tok/s at 1 request against 234 on a community NVLink box
  (`docs/performance.md`, DFlash2, decode-only). Versions, power caps (250 W vs 200 W) and machines differ, so this
  is indicative only.

### Fit for volta-fa

This is a concrete design for the Phase 3 gap in `llama-cpp-volta-baseline.md`: llama.cpp's internal all-reduce is
staged through pinned host memory and never uses P2P.
- **Message size:** a dense 27B with hidden size 5120 moves ~10 KB (FP16) per token per all-reduce, well inside the
  small-message regime.
- **Topology:** Phase 3 is 2 GPUs, so the 4-GPU switch gate does not apply as is. What matters is whether P2P works
  between our two cards; the P-7 probe answers that on day one. If both cards sit under the CPU root complex (PHB)
  rather than a switch, P2P may work but be slower; measure before designing around it.
- **Fusion:** fusing the residual add and RMSNorm into the reduction is a later step for the same kernel.
- **Code:** re-implement from the design; don't import the code.
- Not run.

## 2. D=256 GQA-6 long-prefill attention at 75–76 TFLOPS (breaks our invariants)

`csrc/attention/sm70_79t/` ("79T") and its control `sm70_v37/` target Qwen3.8's attention at TP4: 6 query heads per
KV head, D=256. That is the same shape as our P-6/0003 work.

| | ReVolta 79T (operator, one V100) | llama.cpp + our 0003 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| D=256 GQA 6 prefill | 76.2 TFLOPS @ KV 128K, 75.4 @ 256K (`VALIDATION.md`) | ≈ 47 TFLOPS (`phase0-cloud-2026-10-10.md`) |

ReVolta's end to end figure is 2,712 prompt tok/s cold at 256K on TP4 (one run, eager, no CUDA graphs).

### How it gets there, and why we can't follow

- **Scores in global memory.** v37 keeps a 768 MiB score cache per device. 79T writes 24K-key prefix blocks to a
  workspace sized against the 32 GiB budget. This breaks **INV-NUM-001**.
- **Sampled softmax maxima.** 79T takes one score in eight, adds a 4.0 margin and caps exponents at 10.0. It also
  centres V and scales it by a power of two with 64× headroom. These guards were tuned on that one model ("a
  model-qualified bound, not a proof"); 4× and 16× headroom failed on real requests. This breaks **INV-NUM-002**.
  Its own README calls it "an approximate attention path".
- **Error.** Relative L2 is 0.24% overall and 0.31% on the worst row. Our per-element parity tolerance is
  1e-3 + 2e-3·|ref| (`include/volta_fa/tolerance.h`).
- **Narrow shape.** Q 8000–8192, Hq 6 / Hkv 1, D 256, causal, KV a multiple of 32. KV lengths 8, 16 or 24 tokens
  past a multiple of 32 gave 24–72% sampled error.
- **Code lineage.** 79T is built into vLLM's FA2 extension (`_vllm_fa2_C`). Our hard rules forbid FA-2-derived code.

### What it does tell us

- **Headroom.** At D=256 Volta has ~1.6× more attention throughput available than the fused kernel reaches. The
  fused kernel's limit is register pressure (VKQ held in registers); ReVolta's 128×256 threadblock with 64×64 warp
  tiles and FP32 accumulation runs without spills.
- **Decision needed.** Going that route means changing INV-NUM-001/002: a manager/owner decision, not a ticket.
- **One exact idea to check.** Splitting attention into a mask-free prefix pass and a separately masked causal
  tail. Check whether llama.cpp's MMA kernel already treats fully visible KV tiles as mask-free before counting it
  as a gain.

## 3. sm70 Marlin quantised GEMM with weight repack

`csrc/quantization/marlin/sm70_*` runs GPTQ/AWQ GEMMs on Volta tensor cores. It builds on CUTLASS's SM70
`MmaPipelined` and repacks the weights once (`sm70_gptq_marlin_repack.cu`, `sm70_awq_marlin_repack.cu`). This is a
third independent precedent for load-time weight repacking on Volta, after the superfast fork's QPN matmul and
Hyperkernel (`hyperkernel-ideas-2026-11-06.md`). It adds evidence to the P-9 option (b) decision without changing
the question, which is whether llama.cpp would accept a load-time repack.

## 4. Not applicable

- **DFlash2 / MTP4 speculative decoding, the KV offload tiers, E4M3/INT8 KV formats and prefix caching:** vLLM- and
  model-specific serving features.
- **TP4 PCIe-switch mode and TP8 hierarchical all-reduce:** we have 2 GPUs. The design in §1 carries over; the
  4/8-GPU topologies do not.
- **Cross-device top-1 argmax:** needs vocabulary-sharded logits, which llama.cpp's split modes don't produce.
